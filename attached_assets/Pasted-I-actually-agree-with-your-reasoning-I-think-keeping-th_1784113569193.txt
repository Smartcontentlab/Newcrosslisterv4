I actually agree with your reasoning. I think keeping this as a completely separate micro-tool makes the overall app feel much cleaner.

The “Should I Buy?” tool shouldn’t become a mini listing creator. It should behave more like a calculator or barcode scanner—open it, get an answer, close it.

I’d simplify the philosophy to something like this:

⸻

Should I Buy? Philosophy

This tool is not intended to build inventory or create listings.

Its only purpose is to answer one question:

“If I buy this right now, is it likely worth flipping?”

Everything else belongs in the Listing workflow.

⸻

Speed First

The entire interaction should take around 10–20 seconds.

Open Scanner
↓
Take Photo
↓
Enter Cost
↓
AI identifies item
↓
Quick Google Shopping lookup
↓
🟢 BUY
Listed around $42
👍
Ready for next scan

No saving.

No inventory.

No listing creation.

Just the answer.

⸻

Why use Google instead of sold comps?

Because this isn’t trying to determine the perfect resale value.

It’s trying to answer:

“Do people even appear to be reselling this?”

If the first Google Shopping results show listings for $45–60 and you’re holding it for $6, that’s enough information to make a quick decision.

The detailed research happens later, after you’ve actually bought the item.

⸻

Keep the two tools completely separate

Should I Buy?

* Camera
* Cost field
* AI identification
* Quick Google Shopping lookup
* Green or red recommendation
* Reset

That’s it.

⸻

Create Listing

This is where all the heavier work happens:

* Background removal
* AI descriptions
* Sold comps
* Multi-market pricing
* Profit estimates
* Shipping calculations
* Marketplace optimization
* Crosslisting
* Inventory management

Different purpose, different workflow.

⸻

One addition I would make

The only thing I’d add is a tiny Confidence indicator—not for the user to interact with, but to make the recommendation more trustworthy.

For example:

🟢 BUY

Listed around $42

High confidence match

or

🟡 POSSIBLE BUY

Listed around $35

Low confidence match

If the AI isn’t confident, that’s when it asks for the extra fields (Type, Brand, Keywords). That way, the user only has to do more work when it’s actually necessary.

I think this distinction is one of the strongest parts of your app concept. Most competitors try to make every feature do everything. You’re giving each tool one job and making it fast. The user scans dozens of items while sourcing without cluttering their inventory, then switches to the full listing workflow only after they’ve decided what to purchase. That separation keeps both experiences simple and efficient.
